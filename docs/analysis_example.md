# Analysis Example: Exodus Metaphor Projection

This note records what the demo plots are meant to show. It can be used while revising the paper and as a small GitHub tutorial.

The figures are generated by the reference implementation. They are not exact reproductions of the submitted paper figures.

## How To Generate The Figures

From the repository root:

```bash
python scripts/run_baseline_demo.py \
  --manifest corpus_manifest.example.json \
  --output outputs/figures/semantic_baseline.html \
  --seed 42

python scripts/run_3d_baseline_demo.py \
  --manifest corpus_manifest.example.json \
  --output outputs/figures/semantic_baseline_3d.html \
  --seed 42

python scripts/run_metaphor_projection_demo.py \
  --anchors data/anchors/berlin_liberty_anchor_pairs.csv \
  --manifest corpus_manifest.example.json \
  --output outputs/figures/metaphor_projection.html \
  --seed 42
```

## What The Graphs Represent

Each point is a text chunk or paragraph. The color indicates the source text. In private/local runs, hovering over a point can reveal the corresponding passage. In the hosted GitHub Pages figures, passage text is hidden to avoid redistributing the local research corpus.

The semantic baseline plots show ordinary MiniLM sentence embeddings projected with PCA. These plots ask whether the corpus is organized mainly by source, style, vocabulary, and genre before any metaphor-guided intervention.

The metaphor projection plots show the same corpus projected through a learned two-dimensional contrastive space trained from manually curated liberty-related anchor pairs. These plots ask whether passages become comparable under a shared metaphorical vocabulary of bondage, deliverance, law, covenant, trial, and self-rule.

## Interpretation For Paper Revision

The contrast between the baseline and metaphor-guided projections illustrates the central methodological claim of the study. Generic semantic embeddings tend to preserve differences of source, genre, and vocabulary, making cross-textual conceptual continuities difficult to see. By contrast, the anchor-guided projection reorganizes passages according to a curated metaphorical vocabulary of bondage, deliverance, law, covenant, trial, and self-rule.

In a local historical-corpus run, Exodus and Surah Al-Qasas can be inspected for overlapping structures of divine authority, oppression, trial, and liberation. Modern political texts can be inspected for different transformations of liberty metaphors: Douglass and King around oppression, moral struggle, and delayed justice; Herzl around civic construction, collective destiny, and institutional realization.

These visualizations are exploratory and interpretive rather than conclusive. They identify candidate affinities for close reading and motivate future robustness studies over anchor choice, corpus composition, random seed, and projection stability.

## Possible Captions

### Semantic Baseline

Paragraph-level segments are embedded using a pretrained MiniLM sentence-transformer model and projected into a low-dimensional PCA space. The resulting distribution shows how ordinary semantic representations tend to preserve source-level, stylistic, and lexical differences between texts. This motivates the need for an explicitly metaphor-guided projection rather than relying on generic semantic similarity alone.

### 3D Semantic Baseline

The 3D semantic baseline is the closest didactic counterpart to the paper's initial 3D PCA visualization. It shows the corpus before metaphor-guided training, using three principal components of the MiniLM embedding space. This plot is useful for inspecting whether the corpus separates by source and style before applying the anchor-guided projection.

### Religious Metaphor Projection

Paragraphs from Exodus and Surah Al-Qasas are projected into a learned metaphor space trained from curated liberty-related anchor pairs. The plot is intended as an exploratory map of candidate cross-traditional affinities around divine authority, oppression, trial, deliverance, and the formation of a community under law.

### Modern Political Metaphor Projection

Paragraphs from Herzl, Martin Luther King Jr., and Frederick Douglass are projected into the same anchor-guided metaphor space. The visualization can be read as an exploratory comparison of different modern transformations of Exodus-like liberty metaphors, including oppression, moral struggle, delayed justice, civic construction, and collective realization.
