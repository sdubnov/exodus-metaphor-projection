Exodus Metaphor Projection Analysis Example

This document records what the local historical-corpus demo is meant to show. It can support paper revision and later serve as a small GitHub tutorial.

These plots are generated by the cleaned reference implementation. They reproduce the method and its didactic contrast, not the exact submitted paper figures.

How To Read The Plots

Each point is a text chunk or paragraph. The color indicates the source text. Hovering over a point reveals the corresponding passage.

The semantic baseline plots show ordinary MiniLM sentence embeddings projected with PCA. These plots ask whether the corpus is organized mainly by source, style, vocabulary, and genre before any metaphor-guided intervention.

The metaphor projection plots show the same corpus projected through a learned two-dimensional contrastive space trained from manually curated liberty-related anchor pairs. These plots ask whether passages become comparable under a shared metaphorical vocabulary of bondage, deliverance, law, covenant, trial, and self-rule.

Religious Texts

Religious semantic baseline. Exodus and Surah Al-Qasas are embedded with MiniLM and projected with PCA. This baseline shows the ordinary semantic geometry before metaphor-guided alignment.
Religious metaphor projection. Exodus and Surah Al-Qasas are projected into a learned metaphor space trained from curated liberty-related anchor pairs. The plot is an exploratory map of candidate affinities around divine authority, oppression, trial, deliverance, and the formation of a community under law.

Modern Political Texts

Modern political semantic baseline. Herzl, Martin Luther King Jr., and Frederick Douglass are embedded with MiniLM and projected with PCA. This baseline helps separate ordinary semantic/source organization from metaphor-guided organization.
Modern political metaphor projection. Herzl, Martin Luther King Jr., and Frederick Douglass are projected into the same anchor-guided metaphor space. The visualization supports exploratory comparison of different transformations of Exodus-like liberty metaphors: oppression, moral struggle, delayed justice, civic construction, and collective realization.

Interpretive Summary

The contrast between the baseline and metaphor-guided projections illustrates the central methodological claim of the study. Generic semantic embeddings tend to preserve differences of source, genre, and vocabulary, making cross-textual conceptual continuities difficult to see. By contrast, the anchor-guided projection reorganizes passages according to a curated metaphorical vocabulary of bondage, deliverance, law, covenant, trial, and self-rule.

These visualizations are exploratory and interpretive rather than conclusive. They identify candidate affinities for close reading and motivate future robustness studies over anchor choice, corpus composition, random seed, and projection stability.
