Liberty can mean the absence of interference. A person is free in this sense when no external power blocks an action that would otherwise be available.

Liberty can also mean self-direction. A community or person may seek the discipline required to act according to a rational or moral purpose.

These two ideas can conflict. A law may appear as a restraint, yet also as a condition for shared agency and durable political life.

The danger begins when authority claims to know a person's true will better than the person does. Freedom can then become the language of domination.

The task of political judgment is to ask where constraint protects agency and where it merely disguises coercion.
