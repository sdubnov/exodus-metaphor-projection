A people lived under a ruler who measured their days by command and punishment. Their labor was useful to the city, but their voices were not counted.

A messenger demanded release, not as a private favor but as a moral claim. The demand named bondage as an injury to human dignity.

The journey away from the old power was not only escape. It became a test of memory, trust, fear, and collective discipline.

At the mountain, freedom took the form of obligation. The people learned that release from a master did not yet mean the capacity for self-rule.

The story ended not with a single moment of liberation, but with a community learning how to carry law, memory, and hope together.
